## Current focus projects

- **Scaling production**: from a stated **100+ units/month** towards a target of **1,000 units/month** (no date given).
- **Panther roll-out**: flagship wheeled humanoid with 4WS + 4WD base and 800 mm torso lift; household use is the headline scenario, with commercial sites (unmanned coffee store) as proof points.
- **Wanda 2.0 fleet deployments**: security, hospitality and retail food service are the sectors the company names. Customer names are not public apart from the JD.com Seven Fresh Coffee report.
- **Long-horizon chores as the showcase**: tea-making (about nine chained steps), bed-making, dishwashing, amenity restocking, waste sorting — the same demos recur across CES, the Morgan Stanley summit and WRC.
- **Data flywheel**: the company describes a "data – product – market" model in which robots working at customer sites generate the training data for the next skills.
- **Bipedal line kept alive**: Martian 2.0 (Mar 2026) is the only product with a public promise of development APIs.

## Strategic direction and markets

**Positioning**

- **Form factor bet**: wheeled base + two 8-DoF arms + lifting torso, chosen for uptime, payload and deployment speed over legs.
- **Vertical integration**: arms, gripper (UniPicker Pro) and core components are in-house; modular parts are credited for lower-cost volume production.
- **Three stated pillars**: productisation, globalisation, ecosystem development. An "open ecosystem" is mentioned in passing, with no programme behind it yet.
- **Market sequence**: hotels, property management, security, retail and catering first; **households, elder care and companionship** next; research and education listed as a category.

## What would impress this company at the hackathon

**All of this is inference** from the company's public messaging — not stated by UniX AI.

What the messaging rewards: real service jobs done end to end, long multi-step tasks, reliability without hand-holding, household and care scenarios, and evidence the product travels well outside China.

| Track | Idea that matches their strategy | Why it would land |
|---|---|---|
| **T04 Elderly Care & Rehabilitation** | Panther as a rehab-clinic room assistant: fetch dropped items from the floor, bring tea or water on voice request, tidy the bedside table, alert staff | Uses Panther's unique 800 mm lift and tea-service skill in the "household / elder care" market they name as next but cannot yet reference |
| **T01 Autonomous Urban Services** | Spa-hotel back-of-house loop: restock towels and amenities, wipe surfaces, guide guests, with a task queue from a simple staff app | Direct extension of their medal-winning hotel service tasks; a European hospitality reference is exactly what a globalisation push lacks |
| **T02 Resilient & Safe City** | Night patrol in a clinic or hotel that not only detects (open window, blocked exit, person on the floor) but performs a first intervention | Security is a named deployment sector; adding manipulation to patrol shows why two arms matter |
| **T03 Sustainable City** | Waste sorting or returnables handling at a spa buffet, with measured sorting accuracy | Reuses a skill already demoed at CES; weakest strategic fit, so keep it as a module in a larger system |

Cross-cutting points likely to score with them (inference):

- **A long chain that works three times in a row** beats a flashy single action.
- **Show the data flywheel**: log every run, show how failures become new training examples.
- **European readiness**: German / French voice interaction, privacy-aware perception, clear safety behaviour around elderly guests.
- **Ecosystem evidence**: a clean integration with another vendor on site (edge compute, AR operator view, 3D scan for the map) speaks to their "ecosystem development" pillar.
- **Teach a new skill on site** from a few demonstrations, if they allow it — it validates UniFlex in public.
